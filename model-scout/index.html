How the monthly cost is calculated

Monthly cost = input tokens x input price + output tokens x output price, with prices per million tokens. With a cache share, that share of input tokens uses the cache read price. With batch prices on, models that list a lower batch price use it instead (the cache discount is then not added on top). A rough guide: one page of English is about 500 to 700 tokens.

          4. What to use when

          Rules you can apply without the ranker. Start at the top and stop at the first rule that matches.

          1. Data cannot leave your machines? Use an open-weight model. On a laptop, use a small model (about 8B parameters) for basic chat and extraction. With one strong GPU, a mixture-of-experts model with a few billion active parameters gives much better quality for similar speed. Bigger open models need servers, so price the hardware before you commit.
          2. You need search or similarity, not generated text? Use an embedding model. They cost cents per million tokens. Pick one and stay on it: switching embedding models means re-embedding every document.
          3. High volume, simple answers (classification, tagging, routing, extraction)? Use the cheapest small tier that passes a 50 example test set. Price differences of 10x or more between tiers are common, and simple tasks rarely need a flagship. Ask for short, structured outputs to keep output tokens low.
          4. Answering from your own documents (RAG)? Retrieval quality matters more than model size. Use a mid-tier model and spend effort on retrieval. Only move to long-context models (hundreds of thousands of tokens) when chunking keeps losing the answer, and remember that every token you send is billed.
          5. Whole documents or codebases in one prompt? Filter by context window first: the model must fit your longest input with room for the answer. Then compare input prices, because long prompts are dominated by input cost.
          6. Hard reasoning, tricky code, or work where a mistake is expensive? Use a frontier tier. Check the cost at your real volume: frontier output tokens can cost 50 to 100 times a small tier. A common pattern is a cheap model for the first pass and a frontier model only for the hard cases.
          7. Agents that call tools over many steps? Reliability compounds: a small error rate per step becomes a large one over twenty steps. Favor stronger tiers, and set a cost cap per task.
          8. Real-time chat or autocomplete? Favor fast tiers (names like Flash, Haiku, mini, Lite, Luna). Speed classes here are editorial estimates, so measure time to first token in your own region before you ship.
          9. Still undecided? Pick the cheapest model that passes your test set, then re-run the switch check below every quarter.

                6. Switch triggers: when to reconsider your pick

                • A new model beats yours on your weights. A small score lead is noise. Run a trial when the lead is at least 5 points and the challenger passes your own test set.
                • Pricing changes. Re-run your cost estimate when any provider changes prices. A 2x price cut on a model one tier up can make an upgrade free.
                • Your spec goes stale. Model Scout marks any spec older than 90 days, or with no date, as stale. Re-check stale specs at the source before you rely on them.
                • Your needs change. Longer inputs, a new input type, a privacy requirement, or 10x more volume all change the ranking. Update the profile above and look again.
                • Deprecation notices. Providers retire old versions. Plan the move when the notice arrives, not on the shutdown date.

                7. About the data, and what this app does not do

                • The bundled catalog is dated (loading). Prices and context windows for most models come from OpenRouter's public model list on that date. Buying direct from a provider can cost a different amount.
                • When your browser can reach OpenRouter, Model Scout fetches the live model list and replaces bundled prices with live ones, labelled with the fetch time. If the fetch fails (offline, blocked, or opened from a file with strict browser rules), it uses the bundled catalog and says so at the top.
                • A spec counts as stale when its verification date is missing or older than 90 days. Stale and unverified figures are labelled on every card.
                • Quality tiers and speed classes are editorial estimates from each provider's own positioning of the model (flagship, mid-tier, small). They are not benchmark scores. Model Scout does not publish benchmark numbers.
                • For open-weight models the listed price is what a hosting provider charges. Running the model yourself has no per-token fee, but you pay for hardware and power, which this app does not estimate.
                • Tool calling, JSON output, reasoning mode, cache read prices, batch prices, output limits, and retirement dates come from the same OpenRouter list: its supported parameters, its pricing fields, its ":batch" model variants, and its expiration dates. Models that are not listed there have no capability data, and the app says so.
                • The confidence check on the top pick reruns the ranking 300 times with every factor's weight scaled at random between half and double. It shows how much the pick depends on the exact weights, not how good the model is.
                • What would change the pick moves one weight at a time, with the others keeping their proportions, and solves for the exact share at which another model comes first. Every score is a straight line in that share, so the answer is computed, not sampled. Try it sets those weights by hand so you can see the new ranking; Back to profile weights undoes it.
                • Market watch reads the same public OpenRouter list. New means first listed there in the last 30 days, from the listing date OpenRouter publishes. Price changes compare each reviewed model's live price with the bundled snapshot. Retirements are the expiration dates OpenRouter lists. Free, batch, and other variants are left out. Without the live list it shows only what the snapshot knows.
                • Zero data retention comes from OpenRouter's public list of zero-data-retention endpoints, saved with the catalog and dated. With it required, a hosted model with no listed ZDR endpoint is left out, and a model whose cheapest ZDR endpoint costs more than its listed price is priced at that endpoint. It never lowers a price, since the cheapest endpoint may differ in other ways. Batch prices are set aside, because the list covers standard endpoints only.
                • Split traffic prices sending a share of requests to a cheaper ranked model and the rest to the top pick, assuming requests of similar size. It gives no score for the mix, because that depends on how well easy requests are sorted from hard ones, which this page cannot know.
                • The monthly spend limit is a hard limit: a model whose monthly cost at your volume (with the cache and batch options you chose) is over it is left out, and the excluded list says how much it would cost. Models with no listed price are kept and flagged. In local-only mode it is not applied, because self-hosted cost is hardware, not tokens.
                • The sample counter in the volume helper estimates tokens from text you paste: about 4 characters per token for English and code, about 1 per Chinese, Japanese or Korean character, with runs of spaces counted once. It is a rough count, not a tokenizer. It suggests a cache share only when the repeated opening is at least 1,024 tokens, since providers do not cache short prefixes. The text never leaves the page.
                • What if one constraint changed reruns the ranking once per lever (cache share, batch prices, quality bar, speed target, each required capability, budget, local only, input length) and shows only the changes that move the pick or save 5% or more. It quotes no saving when either side is self-hosted, because that cost is hardware, not tokens. With batch prices and the prompt cache both on, the cost uses whichever is cheaper for your token mix, never both.
                • The ranking runs entirely in your browser. It does not call any model, and Model Scout has no accounts, no server, and no tracking. Your task description never leaves the page. Copy link puts your settings in the page address, never your description.
                • The Ask a model panel is the only part that calls a model, and only when you press Send. With OpenRouter it sends your messages and your own key straight from your browser to openrouter.ai, and OpenRouter and the model provider handle them under their own policies. With Local Ollama it sends them to Ollama on your computer. Your key stays in this browser's local storage; conversations are not saved anywhere and are gone when you reload.
